# Locked evaluation protocol — Gate 1 approval implementation

Specified before development model scores are computed. Data window and primary features are those approved at Gate 1. Seed: 6002026. This file is hashed in the development selection record before final evaluation.

- Primary comparison uses all 371 eligible lagged state-years. Fit expanding histories for validation target years 2019, 2020, 2021. Pool the held-out predictions for model-selection RMSE; every eligible state-year has equal weight. Also report each fold's RMSE, MAE, R² and baseline-relative skill.
- Predictive models: previous-year quantity; historical state mean; log autoregression; log autoregression plus each individual primary lag; all three primary lags with/without state intercepts; pooled untransformed levels; standardized ridge; random forest. Baselines use only earlier targets. Same-year primary regressions are separately labeled retrospective conditional predictions and cannot win the lagged forecast comparison.
- Ridge: standardized continuous predictors, intercept unpenalized, squared-error penalty with lambda 0.01, 0.1, 1, 10, 100 in the sum-of-squares objective. Random forest: 500 trees; mtry 2 or 4; minimum node size 5 or 15; log response; single thread and fixed seed. No additional searches after final evaluation.
- Log-response regressions and forests use training-residual Duan smearing for original-scale mean predictions. Forest in-sample smearing may understate residual spread; disclose it. No clipping of unfavorable errors, observation deletion, or holdout-derived correction. Nonpositive level-regression forecasts are bounded at zero as a prespecified physical constraint and counted.
- Select the simplest eligible candidate whose pooled development RMSE is within 2% of the best. Complexity order: previous-year baseline, historical mean, autoregression, one-indicator regressions, pooled all-indicator regression, ridge, state-intercept regression, random forest. A same-rank tie uses RMSE then stable model ID. This is a pragmatic rule, not a statistical equivalence test.
- Independently choose the best development hyperparameters within ridge/forest for benchmark reporting. Freeze candidate set, selected model, hyperparameters, input/code hashes, and empirical interval calibration before viewing 2022–2023 scores. Final evaluation fits through 2021 for 2022 and refits the unchanged procedure through 2022 for 2023. No retuning between final years.
- Score all frozen benchmark candidates; the primary recommendation follows development selection even if another model looks better on the final years. Report original-scale R², RMSE and MAE, error relative to naive, and change-prediction R². Assess scale dominance with per-year, per-state and training-state-mean-centered metrics.
- Empirical 90% prediction intervals use each model's 5th/95th percentiles of development out-of-fold log(actual/predicted) errors; they are approximate, tuned-development-calibrated intervals without a formal dependent-panel coverage guarantee. Report final empirical coverage and width. State-cluster bootstrap (500 draws) supplies paired final RMSE/MAE differences; only two final years means this cannot quantify future national-shock uncertainty.
- Secondary features and coverage/target transformation sensitivities are evaluated on development folds or clearly separated post-selection explanatory fits. They do not alter final tuning. Compare each secondary subset against its own same-row baseline. Lagged MSHA hours remain excluded from primary claims because of USGS estimation dependence.
- Explanatory analysis: log target on the three log primary contemporaneous predictors plus state/year intercepts. Report state-cluster HC1 uncertainty with t critical values based on number of states minus one; pooled, levels, balanced-coverage, earlier-years, outlay-component, secondary-feature and leave-one-state-out sensitivities. No causal language. Diagnostic concerns limit interpretation rather than trigger holdout chasing.
- Multi-year forecasting feasibility is assessed from sample length, release/vintage limitations, validated one-year skill and unavailable future predictors. No new years, national forecasting model, or future predictor scenarios are silently added.
